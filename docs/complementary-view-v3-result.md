# Complementary-View Optimization V3 Result

## Outcome

V3 establishes the optimization problem and rejects the most tempting but
wrong claim: solving the natural weighted graph objective better does not, by
itself, make retrieval better.

On five exact 18-vertex IRPAPERS slices, multi-start local search reaches the
exact additive optimum in all five. On the complete held-out episodes it also
raises completed edge weight over the original conditional policy. Recall@5
does not rise with it. Across 160 policy/fold/budget observations, additive
edge coverage has Spearman correlation **0.545** with exact full-visual set
agreement but only **0.213** with Recall@5. The bottleneck is objective
sufficiency, not DkS solver quality.

## Query-saturated candidate

The second formulation stops paying linearly for many completed challengers
from one query. Both sparse greedy and multi-start local search agree with the
exact solver on all five small slices. On the complete
source-paper-disjoint evaluation:

| visual-page budget | conditional | sparse saturated greedy | local-search diagnostic | pages |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 81.11% | 81.11% | **81.67%** | 66 |
| 15% | 82.22% | 81.67% | **82.78%** | 100 |
| 20% | 81.67% | **82.78%** | **83.33%** | 135 |
| 25% | 81.11% | **81.67%** | **81.67%** | 169 |

At 20%, sparse greedy is no worse in every fold and recovers one additional
query in two of five folds. It increases exact-teacher agreement from 56.11%
to 57.78%. Its sparse marginal implementation takes about 26 ms across all
five episodes. Multi-start local search recovers one more query and reaches
58.33% agreement, but costs about 1.60 seconds. Sparse greedy is the only
frozen transfer candidate.

It is not yet paper evidence. Query-saturated utility correlates only **0.576**
with exact-teacher agreement and **0.171** with Recall@5. The deployable gain
is two of 180 queries and has no independent confidence on this small
single-gold set. The 26 ms sparse planner is about 17x slower than conditional
selection but remains small relative to tens of seconds of visual
construction; this ratio must be measured, not assumed, on transfer.

## Design insight

The useful insight is narrower than “use graph optimization.” There are two
distinct notions of value:

```text
comparison risk: will visual evidence change this ranking boundary?
task utility:     will changing it improve the retrieval result?
```

Additive DkS optimizes the first and can spend repeatedly on one query. Query
saturation introduces workload fairness and improves the registered point,
but its low Recall correlation shows that fairness alone does not estimate
task utility. A final design needs a deployable what-if estimator or a graded
risk constraint; a stronger combinatorial solver cannot manufacture the
missing signal.

## Contribution status

The repository now contains a reusable sparse optimizer, exact correctness
oracles, prior-art baselines and a leakage-safe evaluator. This is a real V3
system component and a defensible problem formulation. The current evidence
does **not** justify claiming a paper-level selection algorithm.

The next decisive experiment is frozen transfer to official graded ViDoRe HR
and Finance-EN score traces. The server currently retains result summaries but
not the complete `runtime.npz` score surfaces, so they must be regenerated by
the existing official adapter. No new V3 threshold may be chosen after those
results are opened.

## Reproduction

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python tools/analyze_complementary_view_optimization.py \
  --score-surface /path/to/irpapers-full-runtime-score-surface.npz \
  --queries /path/to/irpapers-queries.csv \
  --output results/systems/complementary-view-v3.json
```

The committed result contains fold-level rows, solver times, predictive
validity and exact-oracle ratios. Models, rendered pages and the full score
surface remain outside Git.
